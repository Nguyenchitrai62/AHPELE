# Utility interconnection, energization and permission to operate (PTO)
Typical lifecycle: application -> screening / studies -> interconnection agreement (ISA) -> construction
(customer facilities under an electrical permit from the authority having jurisdiction, AHJ; utility facilities
under a work order paid by the customer) -> AHJ final inspection and approval -> utility commissioning (metering,
telemetry/RTU end-to-end test, POI protective device acceptance) -> witness test or documented waiver -> written
PTO. Parallel operation before PTO happens only for a witness test under a switching order.
Energising part of an installation before final approval (for example for commissioning) is a separate step: take its
conditions from the agreement, the utility's standards, the permit and the switching procedure.
Reviewer's duties:
- Keep the procedural conditions and the engineering acceptability of the installation as separate checks; an approval
  by another party is evidence for both, not a substitute.
- Walk the operating states the project has (construction, commissioning, normal, abnormal, outage, maintenance); in
  each, ask what is energised, what must stay in service and what must be isolated.
- Work to be energized must meet the adopted code as installed.
- Every finding, adverse or favourable, opens and quotes the clause it relies on and checks its conditions and
  exceptions.
- Respect the stated scope.
- Review electrical construction records by NEC article (the edition the permit adopts), plus the utility's
  interconnection standards: 110 (equipment ratings vs available fault current, working space, enclosure ratings,
  markings); 240 (overcurrent protection); 250 (grounding and bonding, equipment grounding and electrode conductors);
  300/310 (wiring methods for the location, support, burial, ampacity with correction factors and terminal ratings);
  312/314 (cabinets, boxes, enclosures); 690 (PV circuits, dc voltage, disconnects, rapid shutdown, labels); 705
  (interconnected sources: point of connection, busbar and conductor rules, disconnects); 706 (energy storage).
Professional reply / memo:
- Answer the question in the first sentence with the decisive reason.
- Open items: for each, the requirement and its source, the evidence in the record, the action, the responsible party
  and the timing.
- Items checked and found acceptable: brief list.
- Internal memo vs external letter: an external letter does not cite internal directives or staff deliberations,
  stays short (1-2 pages), and gives one contact. An internal memo can cite internal procedures.
- Do not raise an obstacle you have not verified.
- Keep tracker entries consistent with the memo or letter.
Before sending: re-check every cited clause and table value against the source; check dates and notice periods
with a script; check that the answer, the open items and the ticket agree; check names and titles against the
contact directory; check that nothing dated after the "as of" date was used.
