# Temporary works and sequencing

Project documents and the adopted regulations govern. When a project document restates a regulation, open the
regulation and apply it as written (or the stricter of the two); a paraphrase is not the requirement.

## Scaffolds and access platforms (US: 29 CFR 1926 Subpart L; check the edition the project adopts)
- Read each paragraph of the subpart that applies (capacity, platform construction, criteria for supported and
  suspended scaffolds, access, use, fall protection, falling-object protection, training) and turn each into a check
  on your own numbers and drawings, for every bay and every erection and dismantling stage.
- Typical checks: component and system capacity against the loads the rule names; platform construction, width and
  gaps; restraint of tall or slender scaffolds (height to base ratio, ties, guys, braces); footing and mudsills;
  guardrails and toeboards; access between levels; clearance from power lines.

## Excavations (29 CFR 1926 Subpart P)
- Protective system for excavations 5 ft deep or more unless entirely in stable rock (sloping, benching, shoring or a
  shield); 20 ft or more needs a design by a registered engineer. Soil type A/B/C and the allowable slopes; water,
  previously disturbed soil and vibration downgrade the soil type.
- Spoil and equipment at least 2 ft from the edge; means of egress within 25 ft of travel in trenches 4 ft deep or
  more; surcharge from adjacent footings and traffic; dewatering effects on neighbours.

## Formwork, shoring, reshoring and partial structures
- Shores carry fresh concrete + construction live load + impact; reshores carry the slabs above until the supporting
  slab reaches the strength the design states; strip only at the stated strength and age.
- Each construction stage is a structure: check stability, bracing and load paths of the partially built frame and
  of existing structures that temporary works bear on or are tied to (anchors, ties, surcharge, vibration).

## Method statement checklist
- Sequence with stage drawings; loads and capacities per stage; temporary works designer and checker; permits and
  inspections (competent person); monitoring and trigger levels; what must be in place before each stage starts.
